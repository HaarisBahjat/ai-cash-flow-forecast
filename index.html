1. WAPE vs MAPE (Why standard MAPE fails)

Daily net cash flows can be zero or cross from positive to negative. Traditional MAPE divides by actual flow, causing infinite explosion when net cash flow is zero or near zero. WAPE (Weighted Absolute Percentage Error: $\sum |y - \hat{y}| / \sum |y|$) weights errors across total turnover, providing a robust, scale-safe percentage.

2. MASE (Mean Absolute Scaled Error)

Formulated by Hyndman & Koehler (2006). Scales the model's MAE against an in-sample one-step naive benchmark. If $\text{MASE} < 1.0$, the model proves statistically meaningful predictive superiority over naive persistence.

3. MDA (Mean Directional Accuracy)

Measures the percentage of days where the forecast correctly identified the flow sign (inflow vs outflow). Crucial for CFOs deciding whether to initiate collections or release supplier payments.

4. Cumulative Balance Path & Liquidity Dip Error

In business liquidity planning, daily errors can offset each other. What determines solvency is the cumulative bank balance path: predicting whether cash dips below safety thresholds, and pinpointing the exact date of maximum crunch.

5. Coverage Probability (PICP) for 80% CI

For an 80% prediction interval, approximately 80% of actual transactions should fall within $[Lower, Upper]$. A model with 50% coverage is dangerously overconfident, while 99% coverage means intervals are excessively wide.

6. RMSE vs MAE for Risk Management

RMSE squares errors, heavily penalizing large unpredicted liquidity shortfalls. A single unforecasted ₹2 Lakh vendor bill triggers overdraft charges or payroll delays; hence minimizing RMSE is paramount.
